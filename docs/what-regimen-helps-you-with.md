# What Regimen Helps You With

> The user-centric statement of what Regimen is for: what it helps a person improve about working with an AI coding agent. Distilled from the problem-space derivation of Regimen's categories of improvement. Intended as the source for the README's opening framing.

## Positioning

Regimen is observability for how you work with an AI coding agent. It turns your gut feel about how a session went into grounded data you can act on, so you get more out of whatever model and harness you are using. It measures the interaction, never your code and never you.

## Who it's for

Anyone working with an AI agent, from their first week to a power user running a big custom kit across four harnesses. It is the same product; your starting point on the curve just changes which answers come back.

## The core job it's hired for

"Tell me how well my AI work is actually going, why, and whether the changes I make to improve it are working, so I stop leaving value on the table and stop guessing."

## The four things it helps you improve

All of them are things you control.

1. **How you set the work up.** Whether a session dragged because the goal, scope, or context wasn't clear up front, and how to state it more clearly next time, or a bit of setup that front-loads that clarity for you.
2. **How you run it.** Whether you are decomposing, delegating, and knowing when to step in or reset, versus letting a run spiral, micromanaging it, or drowning your own context. And when the same thing keeps happening, a skill that runs that kind of work the right way for you.
3. **How you check what comes back.** Whether you actually verified the AI's output or waved it through. This is the one you cannot see in yourself, because you cannot notice a check you never made. And where the check can be mechanized, a gate that runs it for you before you accept.
4. **The durable setup you build around all of it** (your skills, rules, hooks). Whether it is being used, working, missing, stale, fighting itself, or costing more than it saves. And for a beginner: what setup to adopt that they did not know existed.

## Two ways to look at any of it

Over time ("is this getting better; is this thing I keep hitting actually as common as it feels?") and across tools ("does my setup, and my results, hold when I switch model or harness?").

## It tells you why, and it protects you

When something went worse than it should have, Regimen tells you whether it was how you set it up, the AI itself, or your tooling, so you fix the right thing. It explicitly keeps a well-run session that the model or the environment flubbed from looking like your fault. And it flags when a session cost more to steer than it was worth.

## You stay in control

Regimen surfaces the pattern, names the kind of fix, and can offer to help you build it. It never makes the move. You decide, you act.
