# Nightly auto-assessment is a triggered sweep bounded by quiescence and a coverage watermark

> Status: accepted (2026-08-20)

`regimen assess --all` (ADR-0015) makes the judgment layer exist for a whole corpus, but only when an engineer remembers to run it. Left to memory it is not run, and the cost of not running it is not merely a stale verdict: an unjudged conversation eventually becomes an unjudgeable one, because `assess` reads the transcript from the harness files at judge time and harnesses delete those on their own schedule. On the reference store, all 146 conversations whose transcripts have gone are more than thirty days old, matching Claude Code's thirty-day transcript cleanup, and every one of them is a conversation that could have been judged and now never can be. This ADR closes that window by having the capture daemon trigger a nightly sweep, so the judgment layer keeps up with the evidence layer without anyone deciding to make it. It adds no new judging: the thing triggered is the existing sweep over the unchanged per-conversation judge.

## The daemon triggers the sweep, it does not perform it
A judge call takes minutes, reaches an external provider, and fails in ways capture must never inherit. The daemon's own job, draining the capture buffer into the store, is latency-sensitive and must not be blocked behind a metered network call. So the daemon decides that a sweep is due and launches a separate process to run it, then returns to its tick immediately. A sweep that hangs, fails, or is killed costs a night's judging and nothing else; capture is untouched either way. This keeps the trigger a scheduling concern and leaves judging entirely inside the sweep that ADR-0015 already defines.

## Due is a catch-up rule, not a clock
The rule is "has a sweep run today", evaluated on an ordinary daemon tick, rather than "is it 4am now". A laptop that is asleep at the appointed hour would miss a clock-driven sweep silently and forever, and the machines this runs on are laptops that sleep. A catch-up rule sweeps when the machine is next awake, which is the behavior the engineer actually wants: not a sweep at a particular time, but no day without a sweep. It also makes the trigger stateless beyond a single last-swept date, and idempotent across daemon restarts.

## Quiescence is the only honest finished signal, and `session_ended_at` is not one
Selection reuses the existing `list` filter verbatim: `--until 24h` bounds on `last_event_at`, so a conversation is eligible only after a full day of silence. No new selection code is required. The tempting alternative, judging conversations that carry a session-end marker, was tested against the reference store and rejected outright: all 588 conversations carrying an end marker have events recorded after it, one of them 41 days after. A harness emitting SessionEnd means a client disconnected, not that the work is over. Quiescence is the only signal in the store that carries the intended meaning, and it carries it imperfectly, which the next section addresses.

## A full day of quiet, because quiet does not reliably mean finished
Measuring every gap inside every conversation on the reference store shows that a conversation which has been quiet for six hours resumes 31% of the time, at twelve hours 17%, and at a full day still 7%. There is no window that makes quiescence mean finished; there are only windows that make it wrong less often. A full day was chosen over shorter windows because the cost of the sweep is dominated by judging conversations twice: covering the whole reference store once takes 617 calls at the irreducible minimum of one per conversation, and the same coverage costs 627 calls at a one-day window, 650 at twelve hours, and 698 at six. The freshness given up is bounded and small. Because the trigger is nightly rather than continuous, a shorter window mostly buys judging a conversation on the night it ended rather than the following night, and nothing downstream requires a verdict that fast. Verdicts are read on demand, and a day-old verdict answers the questions the read layer asks exactly as well as a fresh one.

## A coverage watermark makes an early judgment recoverable instead of wrong
Given that 7% of conversations wake up after a day of quiet, the sweep will judge conversations that are not finished. Today that error would be permanent: `assessment_run` records that a conversation was judged but not how much of it was judged, so skip-already-judged skips it forever no matter how much it later grows. Migration 11 adds the conversation's `last_event_at` and its event count to `assessment_run` at judge time, and the sweep's due rule becomes: the transcript is present, the conversation has been quiet for a day, and either it has never been judged or it has grown past its watermark. The watermark is therefore load-bearing rather than a refinement, and it is what permits the quiescence window to be a pragmatic choice instead of a search for a correct one. Re-judging itself needs no new machinery, because `--force` already replaces a verdict; the sweep only has to decide which conversations to force.

## A growth threshold, because most regrowth is not worth a second call
A conversation that gains three events after being judged has not changed in any way a verdict would reflect, and re-judging on any growth at all would spend a call to produce the same answer. A conversation is due again only when its new events number at least a quarter of what the previous run covered, with a floor of twenty events, so that a short conversation is not re-judged over trivial regrowth and a long one is not held hostage to a percentage. On the reference store this is the difference between 876 calls and 698 at a six-hour window, and between 660 and 627 at a day: the threshold removes roughly three quarters of the re-judging in every case, which is the cost that made the shorter windows unattractive in the first place.

## Off by default, opted into by config, and capped every night
Automatic assessment spends the engineer's metered judge budget without a person present, which is precisely the thing ADR-0015 refused to do when it made the first batch of a manual sweep the opt-in to spend. So the feature is off unless it is turned on in `~/.config/regimen/env`, alongside the hour and the nightly limit, and never by a CLI flag, consistent with the project's preference for environment configuration over per-invocation flags. The nightly limit is a hard cap on conversations judged per sweep, defaulting to 50. On the reference store the engineer starts about 10 conversations a day on average, 7 at the median, with a busiest day of 28, so 50 absorbs even an unusual day with room over while draining the standing backlog of 168 across four nights rather than firing 168 paid calls the first time the feature is switched on. A backlog that drains over several nights is the intended behavior, not a limitation of it.

## What this does not change
The judge, its rubric, its prompt, and its per-conversation output are untouched. `assess` and `assess --all` behave exactly as they do today when invoked by hand, including the interactive batch pause, which the triggered sweep does not use because no one is watching it. Selection reuses the `list` filters verbatim. Capture, the daemon's drain loop, and the store's evidence-layer tables are untouched. Conversations whose transcripts have already aged out stay unjudgeable; this ADR prevents new members of that class rather than recovering existing ones.

## Considered options
- Judge inline on the daemon tick. Rejected: a metered, minutes-long, externally-dependent call inside the loop that must drain the capture buffer, where its failure modes become capture's failure modes.
- Judge each conversation once, when it first goes quiet, and never revisit it. Rejected as the cheaper option that the measurements disqualified: it judges 7% of conversations on partial evidence at a one-day window and can never correct any of them, because nothing records how much of a conversation a verdict covered.
- Select on `session_ended_at` rather than quiescence. Rejected on evidence: every conversation in the reference store carrying an end marker has events after it, so the marker does not mean what the name suggests.
- A cron entry or a system timer instead of a daemon trigger. Rejected: it duplicates per-OS scheduling machinery the installer already owns for the daemon itself, and a clock-driven job silently skips a sleeping laptop, which the catch-up rule handles by construction.
- A six-hour or twelve-hour quiescence window. Rejected in favor of a day: both spend meaningfully more on re-judging, and the freshness they buy is a single night on work that is read on demand.
- Re-judge on any growth past the watermark. Rejected: roughly four times the re-judging for verdicts that would not differ.
- On by default. Rejected: it spends a metered budget unattended, which is the line ADR-0015 drew and this ADR keeps.
